Data Analysis

Data Preprocessing

The raw dow_jones.csv covers 1901 to 2012. Cleaning steps:

  • DATE parsed as datetime so Pandas can sort and index correctly.
  • Market columns converted to numeric — na strings become NaN.
  • Rows where Close is NaN dropped (non-trading days).
  • Analysis focuses on Close price as the primary target variable.

Transformation

# Log transformation — stabilises variance
log_Close = log(Close)
 
# First difference — removes trend, aids stationarity
ld_Close = diff(log_Close)

First-differencing removes the long-term upward trend, yielding log-returns that fluctuate near zero — a near-stationary series suitable for ARMA and regression models.

Statistical Testing

Stationarity Analysis

ADF Test

Augmented Dickey-Fuller

Small p-value (< 0.05) → reject unit-root H₀ → series is stationary.

KPSS Test

Kwiatkowski–Phillips–Schmidt–Shin

Large p-value (> 0.05) → fail to reject H₀ → series is stationary.

Why stationarity matters: ARMA and linear regression with lags assume constant mean, variance, and autocorrelation. Log-differencing transforms the Close price into log-returns — approximately stationary for equity indices.
